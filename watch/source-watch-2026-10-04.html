What is money, really?

Richard J Murphy · 3rd Oct · https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Rs5fIopDHI8
  • Money is described as a system for recording promises and debts, not a physical thing; banknotes, coins, and account balances are representations of obligations.
  • When a bank makes a loan, it records the borrower’s debt and creates a spendable deposit; when the loan is repaid, those records are cancelled and the created money disappears.
  • Government spending creates money, while taxation removes it; the requirement to settle taxes in pounds creates continuing demand for sterling.
  • Money must not be confused with real wealth, because money cannot build houses, grow food, teach children, or care for people.
Why it matters: It reframes money as accounting for obligations, which changes how one thinks about lending, taxes, and economic capacity.
